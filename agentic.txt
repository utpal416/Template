If you hardcode these prompts inside individual Contact Flows or directly within the AI model’s core instructions, your release lifecycle will become a nightmare, and your manager's AI team will constantly break your IVR code.
To streamline this, you must separate the dynamic data (prompts/branding) from the engine (the IVR code and AI Model).

You should store and configure these prompts in a Dynamic Configuration Database inside your IVR & Orchestration Account.
The Setup:

The Data Store (DynamoDB): You maintain a Partner_Config table in DynamoDB.
The Prompt Ingestion Layer: Instead of hardcoding text, your Connect Contact Flow calls a Lambda function at the very beginning of the call. Lambda fetches the exact verbiage, branding guidelines, and tone requirements for that specific partner from DynamoDB.

The AI Team (The "Reasoning"): They write the system prompt that instructs the model to respect the runtime partner context variables passed by your Lambda. They do not touch the actual partner text files.

Moving from a traditional IVR to an Agentic AI architecture dramatically shifts the responsibilities of Lex and Polly.
They are absolutely still required, but their roles change completely. Instead of being the "brains" of the conversation, they become the "ears, mouth, and nervous system" that connect the caller to the Bedrock Agent.
1. What Happens to Amazon Lex?
In your current IVR, Lex does the heavy lifting of Natural Language Understanding (NLU): it listens to a phrase, matches it to an intent (e.g., ActivateCardIntent), and collects slots.
In the new Agentic AI architecture, the Bedrock Agent handles the reasoning and intent discovery. Lex changes roles depending on which integration path you choose:
Path A: Lex as the "Streaming Conduit" (Most Common Integration)
You do not build complex intents or slots in Lex anymore. Instead, you create a fallback or catch-all intent (like the built-in AMAZON.FallbackIntent or AMAZON.BedrockAgentIntent).
The New Job for Lex: It acts purely as the Automated Speech Recognition (ASR) engine. It streams the customer's voice audio, converts it to raw text, and passes that exact text to your integration Lambda.
Where it goes: It stays in your IVR & Orchestration Account, embedded inside the Connect Contact Flows via the Get customer input block.
Path B: Bypassing Lex Entirely (Direct Connect-to-Bedrock via Contact Flows)
If you are using Amazon Connect's latest native features, you can stream customer speech directly to an orchestration Lambda function or use direct Connect blocks that invoke AI targets without maintaining a separate Lex bot entity at all.
Summary for Lex: You will drastically strip down your Lex bots. You go from managing hundreds of sample utterances and slots to managing a single stream that passes raw text to Bedrock.
2. What Happens to Amazon Polly?
Amazon Polly is 100% required and stays right where it is, but how it gets its text changes completely.
Before: Polly reads hardcoded text prompts that you typed directly into the Amazon Connect Contact Flow blocks (e.g., <speak>Please state the reason for your call.</speak>).
After: Polly becomes dynamic. When the Bedrock Agent processes a turn in the conversation, it outputs a JSON payload containing the text response it wants to say to the customer (e.g., "I have successfully activated your GAP card, Utpal. Is there anything else?").
Where it goes: Your orchestration Lambda receives this text string from the Model Execution account, parses it, and hands it to Amazon Connect's text-to-speech engine. Polly synthesizes that dynamic AI text into speech in real time.

Retaining 64% of your calls inside a traditional IVR is actually a very solid, impressive metric for a standard Lex/Polly setup. It means your current deterministic flows (menus, simple data dips like account balances, and structured forms) are well-optimized.
However, if your current IVR is already doing a great job at the basic, predictable tasks, moving to an Agentic AI architecture isn't about maintaining that 64%; it’s about capturing the remaining 36% and fundamentally changing the user experience.
An Agentic AI IVR provides specific, measurable advancements over a standard Lex/Polly self-service setup:
1. Moving from "Routing" to "Resolution"
Traditional Lex bots are strictly linear and intent-bound. Lex expects the customer to answer specific prompts in a specific order to fill "slots" (e.g., Prompt 1: First Name → Prompt 2: Account Number). If a customer deviates, goes off-script, or asks an unrelated question mid-flow, Lex often breaks, hits a fallback, and transfers the call to a human agent.
The AI Agent Benefit: Bedrock Agents use Large Language Models (LLMs) that understand unstructured, real-world human conversation. A customer can say: "Hey, I want to activate my GAP card, but honestly I forgot my account number and I'm currently walking my dog so I might be a bit distracted."
The Outcome: The AI Agent can handle that messy context, temporarily pivot to look up the account using their phone number, handle the interruption, and seamlessly jump back to the activation journey without failing. This pushes your containment rate beyond 64% by capturing the "messy" callers who usually drop out of traditional IVRs.
2. Eliminating Menu Fatigue and Cognitive Load
With 20+ partners (GAP, Airtel, Priceline), a traditional IVR requires maintaining massive branching trees, distinct sub-menus, or complex Lex bot permutations to ensure customers are handled correctly based on their brand. Callers have to listen to options and adapt to the machine.
The AI Agent Benefit: The AI agent completely flips the control dynamic—the system adapts to the user. Because you feed the partner context (via your DynamoDB design) straight into the agent's brain at the start of the call, the agent instantly adopts that brand's specific persona, vocabulary, boundaries, and logic natively. The customer just speaks naturally, and the agent resolves the issue immediately without making them navigate deep menu trees.
3. Drastic Reduction in Professional Services & Maintenance Costs
Think about how much effort it takes your team today to onboard a single new partner or add a new self-service feature. You have to build new Lex intents, record or configure new Polly prompts, script validation Lambda functions, and redeploy code.
The AI Agent Benefit: To add a new self-service capability (e.g., allowing customers to check order tracking status), you don't build new code paths. You simply attach an API "Tool" (Action Group Lambda) to the Bedrock Agent. The agent inherently figures out when and how to use it based on the flow of the conversation. You go from writing rigid, thousands-of-lines-long decision trees to managing a plug-and-play toolkit.
4. Hyper-Personalization and Deep Data Integration
Traditional Lex can handle simple data entries (like reading back a balance), but it struggles with complex reasoning over backend data.
The AI Agent Benefit: By combining Bedrock Agents with a Knowledge Base (RAG), the agent can cross-reference the customer’s profile, past interaction history, and the specific partner’s current FAQs or dynamic business rules in real time.
Example: If a Priceline customer calls, the agent doesn't just run an activation flow; it can see that their flight was delayed 2 hours ago and proactively say: "I see you're calling about your booking to Chicago. I can help activate your travel profile, and by the way, would you like me to look at your delayed flight options while we're at it?"

Your current IVR is a gatekeeper that deflects 64% of predictable traffic.
